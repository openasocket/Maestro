### The managed and the denied utility turn

The task asks for one managed utility turn and one denied utility turn
through the real endpoint. Neither was run, for two reasons that are both
recorded rather than worked around:

1. The journey catalog (`src/__tests__/integration/mega-maestro/journeys.ts`)
   carries no utility-purpose journey. `launchOnly()` builds every desktop
   snapshot from an attribution of provider, model and resource ids alone, so
   `purpose` is absent and the policy would read it as a conversation. Adding
   a `desktop.utility` journey and its denied twin is authoring, which a
   VERIFY task does not do; it is named below as a Maestro-owned follow-up.
2. Even the conversation journey the catalog has (`desktop.admit`) is denied
   `unauthenticated` by the real endpoint before any policy table is read:
   the registration carries no actor (CP-04a, registration-approval) and no
   clean contracts artifact is pinned (CP-01). A utility turn differs from it
   only in the `purpose` on the snapshot, which the authority never reaches.

The observed zero effect is the container's own: no lease was minted
(`leases: 0`), no journey started a process, the daemon reported no
capability and no policy, and nothing was written outside the container's
private workspace. What P2-02 claims for a utility turn under management
(admitted as `purpose: utility` with `utilityOf` and the `<agent>-ai-<tab>`
trace, denied by a policy with no spawn and no live lease, a ceiling naming
another provider or model refused before the authority is asked, a pinning
ceiling appending `--model` and stripping the user's credential) is proven on
this host only in `context-groomer.managed.test.ts` against the real local
authority behind `fakeSpawnPort`, whose policy answer is a table. That is a
labeled double of the endpoint's decision and is recorded as not passed on
the real pair.
